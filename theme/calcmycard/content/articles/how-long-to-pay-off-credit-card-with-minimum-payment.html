Quick Answer

Paying only the minimum on a typical credit card balance can take well over a decade to reach zero, and can cost more in interest than the original balance itself. The exact timeline depends heavily on your balance, APR, and your issuer's minimum payment formula.

The minimum payment is designed to keep your account current — not to pay off your debt in any reasonable timeframe. Here's what "minimum only" actually looks like across a few realistic examples.

Example 1: A $3,000 balance at 20% APR

Using a common "interest plus 1% of principal" minimum payment formula, a $3,000 balance at 20% APR paid at minimum-only takes about 176 months (roughly 14½ years) to clear in our Minimum Payment Calculator (with a $25 floor), with about $3,980 in interest — more than the original balance.

Example 2: A $7,000 balance at 25% APR

At a higher balance and higher rate, the same minimum-only approach takes about 270 months (roughly 22½ years), with about $13,450 in interest — nearly twice the original balance. This can result in a much longer repayment period and substantially more interest.

Why the timeline is so much longer than people expect

Because the minimum payment recalculates as a percentage of your balance, it shrinks right along with the balance — so the closer you get to paying it off, the smaller your required payment becomes, and the slower your remaining progress gets. This creates a long "tail" at the end of a minimum-only payoff plan that can add years by itself.

The floor payment prevents an infinite timeline — but just barely

Many cards set a fixed dollar floor for the minimum payment (check your cardholder agreement for yours), partly so that very small balances don't take an absurdly long time to clear at a shrinking percentage. Even with that floor, however, minimum-only payoff timelines on real balances remain dramatically longer than most people assume going in.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will my minimum payment ever just become $0?

Not while you still owe more than the floor. Many cards set a dollar floor, so the minimum doesn't drop below that amount while your balance is above it; once the balance is smaller than the floor, the minimum is usually the full remaining balance. Check your cardholder agreement for your card's floor.

What's the fastest way to shorten a minimum-only timeline?

Any fixed amount above the minimum shortens the timeline meaningfully, because it stops shrinking along with your balance the way the minimum does. See the Extra Payment Savings Calculator for an estimate on your own numbers.
