Quick Answer

Most large US issuers set the minimum payment as the greater of a dollar floor ($25 to $41) or 1% of your balance plus that month's interest, plus any late fee or past-due amount. Some also compare it with 2% of the balance. On a $2,500 balance at 22.36% APR, that is about $46.58 in interest + $25 (1%) = $71.58. If your balance is below the floor, the minimum is the whole balance.

Your minimum payment follows a formula written into your cardholder agreement. The formulas differ by issuer, but they are built from the same few pieces. Below are the published formulas of five large issuers and what each one asks you to pay on the same balances.

The general formula

Minimum payment = the greater of: (1% of balance + interest + fees) or a dollar floor
(some cards also compare 2% of the balance, or a fixed amount + interest)

Interest here is the interest charged on that statement. A quick estimate is balance × APR ÷ 12. Past-due amounts are added on top.

On larger balances, Discover's "$20 plus interest" rule asks for the least, which sounds kind but means less goes to principal: on $5,000, only $20 of the $113.17 reduces the balance, against $50 of the $143.17 at the other four issuers.

How to work out your own minimum, step by step

  1. Find your statement balance and the interest charge on your latest statement.
  2. Take 1% of the balance and add the interest and any fees billed this cycle.
  3. Compare that with your card's dollar floor (and 2% of the balance, if your card uses it). The larger number is your minimum.
  4. Add any past-due amount. If the result is more than your balance, the minimum is the balance.

Your exact formula is in your cardholder agreement, usually in the section that explains minimum payments.

Why the formula matters

Because the minimum is tied to a shrinking balance, it shrinks too, which is why minimum-only plans can last 15 to 20 years. Every statement must show how long minimum-only payments would take and what they would cost, under Regulation Z § 1026.7(b)(12). Frequently Asked Questions

Where can I find my card's exact formula?

In your cardholder agreement, which you can view in your online account or request from the issuer. Your statement always shows the actual minimum due.

Can my minimum payment go up even if I don't add new charges?

Yes. A late fee, a past-due amount from a missed payment, or a higher APR (which raises the interest part of the formula) can all push the minimum up.

Is the minimum payment just the interest?

Usually not. Most formulas add 1% of the balance on top of the interest so the balance goes down a little each month. Paying only the interest would leave the balance unchanged.
