Key Finding

Paying only the minimum on a $5,000 balance at 22.15% APR (the Fed's average rate) takes 15 to 20 years and costs $7,474 to $11,529 in interest, depending on which issuer's formula your card uses. Discover's formula is the slowest: 20 years and $11,529. Keeping your payment at your first month's minimum, instead of letting it shrink, cuts payoff to under 5 years and saves more than $4,300.

Every card statement shows a minimum payment, and every major issuer calculates it differently. We ran the published formulas from Chase, Citi, Capital One, Discover and American Express through the same balance and APR to see how long a minimum-only payer stays in debt, and what it costs. Key Findings

  • The lowest minimum costs the most. Discover's formula asks for $30 less in the first month than the others, and that costs about $3,400 to $4,100 more in interest over the life of the balance.
  • The dollar floor sets how long the tail lasts. Chase, Citi and Capital One use the same "interest plus 1% of the balance" formula. Capital One's lower $25 floor (Chase's is $40, Citi's $41) lets payments shrink further at the end, adding about 4 years.
  • Minimum-only payers pay back about $2.50 for every $1 borrowed. With the Chase, Citi or Amex formula you repay about $12,500 on a $5,000 balance; with Discover's, about $16,500.
  • Freezing the payment changes everything. Keeping the first month's $142.29 instead of letting it fall clears the balance in 4 years and 10 months for $3,137 in interest.

The Fix: Freeze Your First Minimum

Minimums shrink as your balance falls, which is why minimum-only payoff takes so long. If you keep paying the amount of your first minimum every month, the whole difference goes to principal.

Methodology

  • Rate: 22.15% APR, the Federal Reserve's Q2 2026 average for credit card accounts assessed interest (G.19, September 8, 2026).
  • Interest: APR ÷ 12 on the balance at the start of each month, the same disclosed model our calculators use. Issuers charge daily interest on the average daily balance, so real figures can differ slightly.
  • Payments: the minimum is recalculated each month from the current balance using each issuer's formula, and the last payment clears the remaining balance.
  • Assumptions: no new purchases, fees, late payments or rate changes. Any of these make payoff longer.
