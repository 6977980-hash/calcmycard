Quick Answer

A balance transfer fee is a one-time charge, commonly 3% to 5% of the amount you move, added to your new card's balance. A $5,000 transfer costs $150 at 3% or $250 at 5%, and issuers can charge it even on a 0% offer. At a 22.36% APR, a 3% fee equals about 1.6 months of interest on the same balance (5% is about 2.7 months), so a transfer usually pays off if you need more than two or three months to clear the debt.

Paying a fee to move debt you already owe can feel odd, but it often costs far less than the interest it replaces. Whether it saves money depends on the fee, your current APR, how long the promotional rate lasts, and how fast you pay. Here are the numbers.

How the fee is charged

The fee is a percentage of the amount transferred, sometimes with a dollar minimum (whichever is greater), and it is added to your new balance on the day of the transfer. Move $5,000 with a 3% fee and you start at $5,150. The fee is not interest, so a 0% promotional APR doesn't waive it. Under Regulation Z § 1026.55, a promotional rate must last at least six months, and the issuer has to tell you the rate that applies after it ends.

The break-even rule

Divide the fee rate by your current monthly interest rate (APR ÷ 12). The result is how many months of interest the fee is worth. At 22.36% APR, the Federal Reserve's Q3 2026 average for accounts charged interest, the monthly rate is about 1.86%:

  • 3% fee: 3 ÷ 1.86 ≈ 1.6 months of interest
  • 5% fee: 5 ÷ 1.86 ≈ 2.7 months of interest

If you would clear the balance in less time than that anyway, the fee likely costs more than it saves.

When the fee might not be worth it

  • You can pay it off in a month or two. As the 3-month row shows, a 5% fee can cost more than the interest.
  • The promo is shorter than your payoff plan and the card's regular APR is as high as your current one.
  • You keep spending on the new card. New purchases may not get the 0% rate and can lose the grace period while a transferred balance remains.
  • It's a deferred-interest offer, not a 0% APR. A store card's "no interest if paid in full" deal charges all the interest back to the purchase date if any balance is left. The Deferred Interest Calculator shows what that could cost.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the transfer fee charged interest?

The fee becomes part of your balance, so it is covered by the promotional APR while that lasts. If any of it is still unpaid after the promo ends, it is charged the regular APR like the rest of the balance.

Is the transfer fee tax-deductible?

Tax treatment can depend on how the debt and fee are used; consult current IRS guidance or a qualified tax professional.

Can I negotiate the transfer fee?

Sometimes, especially with a strong credit profile or when an issuer is actively running a promotion. It doesn't hurt to ask, but there's no guarantee. Some cards advertise a lower fee for transfers made in the first 60 days.
