Quick Answer

On a deferred-interest offer ("no interest if paid in full within 12 months"), interest builds up at the card's APR from the purchase date. Pay the whole promo balance by the deadline and it is never charged; leave even $1 and all of it is added at once. To be safe, pay at least the purchase amount ÷ the promo months, and finish a month early.

Deferred interest is common on store credit cards and retail financing for furniture, electronics, appliances and medical bills. It looks like a 0% offer, but it isn't one: a true 0% intro APR charges interest only on what's left after the promo, while a deferred-interest plan charges back interest on the full purchase, from day one. This calculator shows how much interest is building up behind the "no interest" label and what you'd pay if you miss the deadline.

How the Calculator Works

Enter the purchase amount, the length of the promotion, the card's APR and your monthly payment. The calculator:

  1. Divides the purchase by the promo months to find the payment that clears it in time, rounded up to the cent.
  2. Runs the promotion month by month: interest builds at APR ÷ 12 on the promo balance, then your payment reduces the balance. The interest is tracked separately, not added to the balance.
  3. If the balance reaches $0 within the promotion, the built-up interest is waived. If not, all of it is added to what you still owe.
  4. Runs that new balance through the monthly model at the card's APR, at the same payment, to show the total interest.

Formula

Payment to Avoid Deferred Interest = Purchase ÷ Promo Months
Deferred Interest = sum over each promo month of (Promo Balance × APR ÷ 12)
If any balance is left at the deadline: New Balance = Balance Left + Deferred Interest

This is a simplified monthly estimate. Issuers usually accrue deferred interest daily on the average daily balance, so your statement figure may differ by a few dollars.

How to Avoid Paying Deferred Interest

  • Pay more than the minimum. The required minimum on a deferred-interest plan is often too small to clear the balance in time. The CFPB warns that paying only the minimum can leave you owing the deferred interest.
  • Finish a month early. The promotion's end date is printed on your statement and may not match your payment due date. Aiming one month ahead leaves room for a payment that posts late.
  • Don't be more than 60 days late. The CFPB notes that a payment more than 60 days late can end the promotion early and trigger the deferred interest.
  • Watch other balances on the same card. Payments above the minimum normally go to the highest-APR balance first, but under Regulation Z § 1026.53, in the last two billing cycles before a deferred-interest promotion ends, the issuer must put the excess toward the deferred-interest balance first. Before that, new purchases can soak up payments you meant for the promo.
  • Consider moving it. If you can't clear it in time, a 0% balance transfer before the deadline may cost less; compare with the Balance Transfer Calculator.

When the Result May Differ

Treat the results as a planning estimate. Your real numbers can differ because of:

  • Daily accrual: issuers usually calculate deferred interest daily on the average daily balance, and the timing of your payments within each cycle changes the total.
  • Several promotions on one card: store cards often carry more than one promotional purchase, each with its own deadline, plus regular purchases at the standard APR.
  • Fees: late fees and other charges aren't included. See Credit Card Late Fees Explained.
  • New purchases: the estimate assumes the promo purchase is the only balance.
