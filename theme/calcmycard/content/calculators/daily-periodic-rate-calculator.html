Quick Answer

A daily periodic rate is typically your APR divided by 365 (some cards use 360). Multiply it by your average daily balance and the number of days in the billing cycle to estimate the interest for that cycle.

Many issuers don't apply your APR directly. They use a small daily slice of it, applied to your balance each day of the billing cycle. This calculator shows that daily rate and an estimate of what it costs.

How the Calculator Works

Enter your APR, your average daily balance, the number of days in your billing cycle, and whether your issuer uses a 365- or 360-day year. The calculator returns:

  • Daily periodic rate: APR ÷ 365 (or 360).
  • Interest per day: average daily balance × daily rate.
  • Interest this billing cycle: interest per day × days in cycle.
  • Effective APR with daily compounding: (1 + daily rate)365 − 1.

Unlike the site's payoff calculators, this one uses the daily method rather than the simplified monthly model.

Formula

Daily Periodic Rate = APR ÷ 365 (or 360)
Cycle Interest = Average Daily Balance × Daily Periodic Rate × Days in Cycle
Effective APR = (1 + Daily Periodic Rate)365 − 1

This is an estimate. Issuers differ in how they compound (daily vs. once per cycle), which balances they include, and how they round, so your statement may differ.

How to Find Your Average Daily Balance

Add up your balance at the end of each day in the cycle and divide by the number of days. If you owed $2,500 for 10 days and $3,250 for 20 days of a 30-day cycle: (10 × $2,500 + 20 × $3,250) ÷ 30 = $3,000. A payment made early in the cycle lowers the average for more days than the same payment made just before the due date.

When the Result May Differ

This calculator uses the daily method, but your statement can still differ because of:

  • Fees: annual, late, foreign-transaction, cash-advance, and balance-transfer fees aren't included unless the calculator asks for them.
  • Grace period: whether new purchases accrue interest depends on whether you pay your statement balance in full and on your card's terms.
  • Different APR categories: purchases, balance transfers, cash advances, and penalty APRs can carry different rates on the same card, and how payments are allocated between them affects the total.
  • Compounding method: some issuers add each day's interest to the balance (daily compounding); others total it at the end of the cycle. This calculator shows simple daily interest for the cycle.
  • Which balances count: whether new purchases are included in the average daily balance depends on your card's terms and grace period.
  • Rounding and minimum interest charges: issuers may round daily rates or charge a minimum interest amount.
