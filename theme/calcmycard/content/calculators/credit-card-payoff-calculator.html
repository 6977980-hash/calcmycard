Quick Answer

Your estimated payoff date depends on three things: your balance, your APR, and your fixed monthly payment. The calculator above projects the month you'd reach zero, your total interest, and how much sooner an extra $50 a month would get you there.

"How long is this going to take?" is the question most people carrying card debt want answered — not an abstract rate, but a date on the calendar. This calculator estimates that date, plus the total cost of getting there.

How the Calculator Works

Enter your current balance, APR, and the fixed amount you plan to pay every month. The calculator runs a month-by-month simulation: each month it adds interest on the remaining balance (APR ÷ 12), applies your payment, and carries the new balance forward. It stops when the balance hits zero and reports:

  • Your estimated payoff date and number of months
  • Total interest and total amount paid
  • A chart of your balance declining over time
  • An automatic comparison showing what adding $50 a month would change

Formula

Monthly Interest = Balance × (APR ÷ 12)
New Balance = Balance + Monthly Interest − Payment
Repeat until New Balance ≤ 0 → months to payoff

This is a simplified estimate, not an issuer-specific statement calculation. A closed-form version is n = −ln(1 − B·r ÷ P) ÷ ln(1 + r), where r = APR ÷ 12, B = balance, and P = payment.

Why Payoff Isn't a Straight Line

Because interest is charged on whatever balance remains, early payments are mostly interest and later payments are mostly principal. That's why a balance can feel stuck for the first year before it starts dropping visibly — the table above shows the principal share growing each month.

When the Result May Differ

Treat the results as a planning estimate. Your real numbers can differ because of:

  • Daily periodic rate: many issuers apply APR ÷ 365 (or 360) each day instead of APR ÷ 12 once a month, which can produce different interest.
  • Average daily balance: your balance moves during the cycle as purchases post and payments arrive; this calculator uses a single month-start balance.
  • Fees: annual, late, foreign-transaction, cash-advance, and balance-transfer fees aren't included unless the calculator asks for them.
  • New purchases: the estimate assumes you stop adding charges. Any new spending lengthens payoff and raises interest.
  • Grace period: whether new purchases accrue interest depends on whether you pay your statement balance in full and on your card's terms.
  • Different APR categories: purchases, balance transfers, cash advances, and penalty APRs can carry different rates on the same card, and how payments are allocated between them affects the total.
  • Payment changes: the calculator assumes the same payment every month. Skipped, late, or larger payments change the date.
