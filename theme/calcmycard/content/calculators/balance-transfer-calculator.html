Quick Answer

A balance transfer may be worth it when the interest you'd save is greater than the transfer fee (often 3-5% of the amount moved). The calculator above compares the total cost of staying on your current card with the cost of transferring, fee included, at the same monthly payment.

"0% APR for 15 months" looks like an obvious win, but the fee changes the math — and if you can't pay off the balance before the promo ends, the savings can shrink quickly. This calculator nets everything out before you apply.

How the Calculator Works

Enter your balance, your current APR, what you can pay each month, and the offer's terms: fee percentage, promotional APR, promo length, and the APR after the promo ends. The calculator then runs two simulations at the same monthly payment:

  1. Stay put: your balance paid down at your current APR.
  2. Transfer: the fee is added to the balance, the promotional rate applies for the promo months, and the go-to APR applies afterward.

Your estimated net savings = interest if you stay − (interest after transferring + transfer fee).

Formula

Transfer Fee = Balance × Fee %
Transferred Balance = Balance + Transfer Fee
Monthly Interest = Balance × (Promo APR ÷ 12) during the promo, then × (Go-to APR ÷ 12)
Net Savings = Interest (stay) − [Interest (transfer) + Transfer Fee]

This is a simplified estimate, not an issuer-specific statement calculation.

Example: $6,000 at 24.99% APR, paying $300 a month

The offer: 3% fee, 0% for 15 months, then 24.99%. The fee is $180, so the new balance starts at $6,180.

When the Result May Differ

Treat the results as a planning estimate. Your real numbers can differ because of:

  • Daily periodic rate: many issuers apply APR ÷ 365 (or 360) each day instead of APR ÷ 12 once a month, which can produce different interest.
  • Average daily balance: your balance moves during the cycle as purchases post and payments arrive; this calculator uses a single month-start balance.
  • New purchases: the estimate assumes you stop adding charges. Any new spending lengthens payoff and raises interest.
  • Different APR categories: purchases, balance transfers, cash advances, and penalty APRs can carry different rates on the same card, and how payments are allocated between them affects the total.
  • Promo terms: under federal rules an issuer can generally end a promotional rate on your existing balance early only if your minimum payment is more than 60 days late (Regulation Z § 1026.55), and some offers (especially retail financing) use deferred interest, which can charge interest back to the start if the balance isn't paid in full by the deadline.
  • Fee structure: some cards charge a percentage with a minimum dollar amount; others charge a different fee after an introductory window.
  • Credit limit: the new card's limit may be lower than the amount you want to move, which would leave part of the balance on the old card.
  • New purchases on the new card: these may accrue interest at the purchase APR and may not get a grace period while a transferred balance remains, depending on the card's terms.
