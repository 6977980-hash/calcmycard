Quick Answer

Credit utilization is your card balance divided by your credit limit. Lower is generally better. Staying under 30% is a common rule of thumb, but FICO says there's no sudden drop at 30%, and its data shows people with the highest scores use a much smaller share of their limits than average. The calculator above shows your per-card and overall ratio.

Credit utilization is one of the most influential factors in common credit scoring models, and unlike payment history it can change significantly from one statement to the next. This calculator shows where you stand, card by card and overall.

How the Calculator Works

Add each card's name, current balance, and credit limit. The calculator shows:

  • Utilization for each card (balance ÷ limit)
  • Overall utilization (total balances ÷ total limits)
  • A general rating band for each card and overall
  • How much you'd need to pay down to reach 30% and 10% overall

There's no interest math involved, so the monthly-interest method used elsewhere on this site doesn't apply here.

Formula

Card Utilization = Card Balance ÷ Card Limit × 100
Overall Utilization = Total Balances ÷ Total Limits × 100
Pay-down to reach X% = Total Balances − (Total Limits × X%)

The 30% and 10% bands are general guidelines, not thresholds set by any scoring company.

When the Result May Differ

Your ratio here uses the numbers you type in. What scoring models see can differ because of:

  • Reporting date: issuers usually report the balance on your statement closing date, not the balance on the day you check or pay.
  • Which accounts count: some models treat closed cards, charge cards without a preset limit, or authorized-user accounts differently.
  • Scoring model differences: FICO and VantageScore versions weigh overall and per-card utilization differently.
  • Limit changes: a lowered credit limit raises your ratio even if your balance doesn't change.

Your credit reports show the balances and limits lenders actually reported.

The Fastest Way to Lower It

Because utilization is usually a statement-date snapshot, paying down a balance before the statement closes can lower your reported ratio within a single cycle. If high utilization reflects debt you're paying down, the Multi-Card Debt Payoff Calculator can help you choose an order.
