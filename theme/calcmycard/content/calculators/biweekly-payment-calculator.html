Quick Answer

Paying half your monthly credit card payment every two weeks means 26 half-payments a year — the same as 13 monthly payments instead of 12. On a $5,000 balance at 22.99% APR, paying $100 every two weeks instead of $200 a month clears the card about 4 months sooner and saves about $248 in interest. Most of that saving comes from the extra payment, not the timing.

Bi-weekly payments are often suggested for mortgages, and the same idea works on a credit card. This calculator shows how much it really saves on your balance, and how much of that you'd get just by paying a little more each month.

How the Calculator Works

Enter your balance, APR, and the monthly payment you'd normally make. The calculator runs two plans side by side:

  • Monthly: interest at APR ÷ 12 each month, then your full payment.
  • Bi-weekly: interest at APR × 14 ÷ 365 every two weeks, then half your payment.

It reports how long each plan takes, the interest each costs, and the difference.

Formula

Monthly plan: Balancenext = Balance × (1 + APR ÷ 12) − Payment
Bi-weekly plan: Balancenext = Balance × (1 + APR × 14 ÷ 365) − Payment ÷ 2

A simplified model: issuers charge daily interest on your average daily balance, so real savings from earlier payments depend on your card's billing cycle.

Bi-weekly saves $248, but paying the same $2,600 a year in monthly payments already saves $208 of it. The real lever is paying more; bi-weekly is simply an easy way to do that if you're paid every two weeks.

When the Result May Differ

  • Daily interest: your issuer charges interest on your average daily balance, so the benefit of paying earlier depends on when your payments land in the cycle.
  • Payment posting: confirm that your issuer accepts more than one payment per cycle and applies it to the balance right away.
  • Minimum payment: make sure at least the minimum is paid by each due date; two half-payments that straddle a due date can leave you short.
  • New purchases lengthen payoff under either plan.
