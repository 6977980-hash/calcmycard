Quick Answer

A credit card cash advance usually costs a fee of 3–5% of the amount (often with a $10 minimum) plus interest at the cash advance APR from the same day, with no grace period. For example, a $500 cash advance with a 5% fee at 29.99% APR, repaid after 30 days, costs about $40.94 including a $3 ATM fee — roughly 100% a year.

A cash advance looks like a simple withdrawal, but it is one of the most expensive ways to use a credit card. This calculator adds up the fee, the interest, and any ATM fee so you can see the real cost before you take the cash.

How the Calculator Works

Enter the amount, your card's cash advance APR, the fee terms, how many days you expect to keep the money, and any ATM fee. The calculator then:

  • Works out the fee as the fee percentage of the amount, or the minimum fee if that is higher.
  • Adds the fee to the balance, since issuers usually add it to what you owe.
  • Charges daily interest on that balance at the cash advance APR ÷ 365 for every day until you repay it.
  • Adds the ATM operator fee, if any, and shows the total and the annualized cost.

Formula

Fee = max(Amount × Fee %, Minimum Fee)
Interest = (Amount + Fee) × (Cash Advance APR ÷ 365) × Days
Total Cost = Fee + Interest + ATM Fee
Annualized Cost = (Total Cost ÷ Amount) × (365 ÷ Days)

This is a simplified estimate using simple daily interest with no compounding, not an issuer-specific statement calculation.

Example: $500 cash advance, repaid after 30 days

The card charges a 5% fee with a $10 minimum and a 29.99% cash advance APR, and the ATM charges $3. The fee is $25 (5% of $500 is more than $10), so the balance becomes $525, which builds about $0.43 of interest a day.

When the Result May Differ

Treat the results as a planning estimate. Your real numbers can differ because of:

  • Compounding and billing cycles: issuers may compound interest daily and add it to your balance each cycle, which raises the cost slightly on longer advances.
  • Interest on the fee: most cards add the fee to the cash advance balance, as this calculator does; some may treat it differently.
  • Payment allocation: if you also carry purchases, your minimum payment may go to the lower-APR balance first, which can keep the cash advance accruing longer.
  • Cash-like transactions: convenience checks, money transfers, and some gift card or cryptocurrency purchases can be treated as cash advances.
  • Cash advance limits: many cards set a separate, lower limit for cash advances.

Cheaper Ways to Get Cash

Before taking a cash advance, compare it with a debit card withdrawal from your own account, a small personal loan or credit union loan, or simply using the card for the purchase itself so it gets a grace period.
