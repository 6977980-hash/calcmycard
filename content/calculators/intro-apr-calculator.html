Quick Answer

Divide your balance by the number of months in your 0% promotional period to find the flat payment that clears the balance before the promo ends. The calculator above does that math, checks your planned payment, and estimates the cost of any balance left over.

A 0% intro APR offer only avoids interest if you pay off the balance before the promotional period ends. This calculator tells you the payment you'd need, and what could happen if you fall short.

How the Calculator Works

Enter your balance, the number of promotional months, your planned monthly payment, and the APR that applies after the promo. The calculator:

  1. Divides the balance by the promo months to find the required flat payment.
  2. Multiplies your planned payment by the promo months to see how much you'd pay off during the promo.
  3. Calculates any remaining balance when the promo ends.
  4. Estimates the interest on that remainder at the go-to APR (monthly model) if you keep paying the same amount.

Formula

Required Payment = Balance ÷ Promo Months
Remaining Balance = max(0, Balance − Planned Payment × Promo Months)
After the promo: Monthly Interest = Remaining Balance × (Go-to APR ÷ 12)

This is a simplified estimate, not an issuer-specific statement calculation. It assumes a true 0% promotion, not a deferred-interest plan.

Example: $3,000 on an 18-month 0% offer, paying $150 a month

Simplified monthly model; true 0% promotion assumed.
ItemAmount
Required payment ($3,000 ÷ 18)$166.67
Paid during the promo ($150 × 18)$2,700.00
Balance left when the promo ends$300.00
Estimated interest on the remainder at 24.99%, paying $150/mo$9.70

Here the shortfall is small, so the extra interest is small too. On a deferred-interest offer, though, missing the deadline could mean interest charged back to the original purchase date on the full amount — a very different result. Paying $166.67 a month avoids both.

When the Result May Differ

Treat the results as a planning estimate. Your real numbers can differ because of:

  • Fees: annual, late, foreign-transaction, cash-advance, and balance-transfer fees aren't included unless the calculator asks for them.
  • New purchases: the estimate assumes you stop adding charges. Any new spending lengthens payoff and raises interest.
  • Different APR categories: purchases, balance transfers, cash advances, and penalty APRs can carry different rates on the same card, and how payments are allocated between them affects the total.
  • Deferred interest: some promotions (common with store cards and retail financing) charge interest retroactively if the balance isn't paid in full by the deadline. This calculator assumes a standard 0% promotion.
  • Losing the promo early: under federal rules an issuer can generally end a promotional rate on an existing balance early if your minimum payment is more than 60 days late (Regulation Z § 1026.55), and on deferred-interest plans being more than 60 days late can trigger the deferred interest.
  • Minimum payments: your card's required minimum may be lower than the payment needed to clear the balance in time; paying only the minimum could leave a larger remainder.
  • Payment allocation: if the card also carries balances at a higher APR, amounts above the minimum generally go to the highest-APR balance first, which can slow payoff of the promo balance.
  • Daily interest after the promo: once the go-to APR applies, many issuers use a daily periodic rate on the average daily balance.
