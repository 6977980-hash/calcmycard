Quick Answer

Common minimum-payment formulas include a percentage of the balance, interest plus a percentage of principal, or a combination of these methods, often with a fixed dollar floor. Your cardholder agreement determines the actual formula. The calculator above estimates your minimum under common formulas and shows how long paying only the minimum could take.

Your minimum payment is the smallest amount your issuer will accept without a late fee — and it's set low. Understanding how it's calculated, and what happens if you never pay more than it, is one of the most useful things to know about your card. To see how much of any fixed payment goes to interest, use the Credit Card Interest Calculator.

How the Calculator Works

Pick your card issuer to load its standard formula, or choose a formula yourself, then enter your balance, APR, the percentage your card uses, and its dollar floor. The calculator offers two common structures:

  • Interest + 1% of balance: this month's estimated interest plus 1% of the balance, compared with the percentage you enter, whichever is greater.
  • Flat percentage: a fixed percentage of the balance.

Either result is raised to the dollar floor if it falls below it. The calculator then simulates paying only the recalculated minimum each month — the minimum shrinks as the balance shrinks — and reports the payoff time, total interest, and total paid.

The issuer presets use each issuer's standard published formula, listed below. Your specific card may use a different percentage, add fees or past-due amounts, or combine methods differently.

Formula

Interest-based: Minimum = max(Balance × P%, Balance × (APR ÷ 12) + Balance × 1%)
Flat: Minimum = Balance × P%
Either way: Minimum = max(Minimum, Dollar Floor)

When the Result May Differ

Treat the results as a planning estimate. Your real numbers can differ because of:

  • Daily periodic rate: many issuers apply APR ÷ 365 (or 360) each day instead of APR ÷ 12 once a month, which can produce different interest.
  • Average daily balance: your balance moves during the cycle as purchases post and payments arrive; this calculator uses a single month-start balance.
  • Fees: annual, late, foreign-transaction, cash-advance, and balance-transfer fees aren't included unless the calculator asks for them.
  • New purchases: the estimate assumes you stop adding charges. Any new spending lengthens payoff and raises interest.
  • Different APR categories: purchases, balance transfers, cash advances, and penalty APRs can carry different rates on the same card, and how payments are allocated between them affects the total.
  • Your card's actual formula: percentages, floors, and whether fees, past-due amounts, or over-limit amounts are added vary by card and are set out in your cardholder agreement.
  • Rounding and statement timing: issuers may round the minimum and calculate it from your statement balance rather than a month-start balance.

Your statement and cardholder agreement are the final word. See our methodology for how every calculator on this site works.

Where to Find Your Actual Minimum

Your real minimum payment is printed on every statement, and the formula is in your cardholder agreement (look for the section on minimum payment calculation). U.S. card statements must also include a minimum payment warning showing about how long payoff would take, and what it would cost, if you paid only the minimum (Regulation Z § 1026.7(b)(12)).
