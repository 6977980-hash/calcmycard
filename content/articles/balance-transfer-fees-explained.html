Quick Answer

Balance transfer fees are commonly 3% to 5% of the amount transferred, charged once at the time of transfer, and card issuers are allowed to charge them even on 0% offers. Despite the upfront cost, a transfer may still be worth it if you're carrying a high-APR balance for more than a few months.

A balance transfer fee can feel like a strange cost to accept — paying money to move debt you already owe — but it can still save money when the alternative is months or years of high-interest charges. Whether a transfer saves money depends on the balance, current APR, transfer fee, promotional period, payment amount, and post-promotion APR.

How the fee is typically charged

The fee is usually a percentage of the transferred amount (commonly 3% to 5%), sometimes with a minimum dollar amount (whichever is greater), added directly to your new card's balance at the time of transfer. A $5,000 transfer at 3% adds a $150 fee, bringing your new starting balance to $5,150.

The simple math for deciding if it's worth it

Compare the fee against the interest you'd otherwise pay on your current card over the same period. If your current APR is high (20%+) and you're planning to carry the balance for more than 3-6 months, the interest saved will often exceed a 3-5% one-time fee — but run your own numbers, because a short payoff timeline or a small APR gap can make the fee the bigger cost.

A worked example

On a $5,000 balance at 24% APR, carrying it for 12 months at a $450/month payment would cost roughly $600-$700 in interest. A transfer with a $150 fee (3%) to a 0% promotional card, paid off over the same 12 months, costs just that $150 fee — a savings of several hundred dollars.

When the fee might not be worth it

If you can pay off your current balance within just a month or two anyway, the interest you'd naturally avoid may be smaller than the transfer fee itself — in that specific case, staying put and paying it off directly can be cheaper. The fee also matters more on very large balances, where even a small percentage becomes a large dollar amount.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the transfer fee tax-deductible?

Tax treatment can depend on how the debt and fee are used; consult current IRS guidance or a qualified tax professional.

Can I negotiate the transfer fee?

Occasionally, especially with a strong credit profile or during a promotional period when an issuer is actively trying to win your business — it doesn't hurt to ask, but it's not guaranteed.
